# Investor Canon — Distilled Doctrine (optional judgment aid)

Optional background the scanner can draw on when scoring candidates or writing the BEGINNER
SUMMARY. None of this overrides SKILL.md's gates — it's judgment seasoning, not a rulebook.

## On trend and relative strength (Minervini / O'Neil lineage)
- Buy strength, not weakness. The best-performing stocks over the next few months are usually
  already among the better performers of the last few months — this is why the trend template and
  relative-strength ranking are the primary Genesis filter, not a contrarian "buy the dip" reflex.
- A stock in a proper uptrend (price > rising 200-DMA > 150-DMA, making higher highs) has already
  proven itself; a stock still below its long-term average hasn't, no matter how cheap it looks.
- Cut losses fast and let winners run. The ~10% initial stop and the uncapped trailing runner
  encode this directly: the maximum loss on any one idea is bounded tightly, the maximum gain is
  not bounded at all.

## On position sizing and risk (Turtle Traders lineage)
- Position size is the real risk control, not the stop. A stop can gap through on bad news or a
  weekend headline; the position being small enough that a full loss doesn't hurt is what actually
  protects the account. This is why SKILL.md never allows "up-sizing to compensate" for wider
  stops, and why per-trade risk is capped as a percent of equity, not a fixed dollar amount alone.
- Pyramid into strength, never into weakness. Adding to a position that's working, at a measured
  interval (ATR-based), compounds a good idea; adding to a position that's down is averaging into
  a mistake — hence the hard "never average down" rule.
- Diversify entries across time and instruments, but don't over-diversify a small book. 3-8
  positions is enough to smooth single-name risk without diluting the size of the best ideas to
  the point they don't matter.

## On capital preservation first (general risk-management doctrine)
- The first job of a trading system is to survive long enough for the edge to play out. A single
  bad day that takes the account down 20% requires a 25% gain just to get back to even — this is
  the reasoning behind the daily-loss circuit breaker and the consecutive-loss pause: after a
  cluster of losses, the right move is to stop and check the assumptions, not to keep firing.
- Avoid forcing a trade. On a day with no qualifying setup, the correct trade count is zero. A
  scanner that always finds something to buy is optimizing for activity, not for return — this is
  the whole reason the default output is NO TRADE.
- Binary-event risk (earnings, FDA decisions, litigation rulings) is a different kind of risk than
  trend risk — no amount of technical quality protects against a 20% overnight gap on an earnings
  miss. This is why the earnings guard is a hard block, not a scoring input.

## How to use this file
When scoring a candidate or explaining a decision in the BEGINNER SUMMARY, it's fine to reference
these ideas in plain language ("this stock is a leader, not a laggard, so we're comfortable owning
it here" / "we're pausing today because two of our last three trades got stopped out — that's the
system telling us to slow down and double check, not bad luck to trade through"). Never let this
file's doctrine talk you into loosening a SKILL.md gate — if the two conflict, SKILL.md wins.
